Part 1

How to Write the Perfect Resume

1

Pick the Right Format

Your format decides how recruiters read your story. Choose wisely:

📋 Chronological

Best for: steady career progression

Lists jobs newest → oldest. The #1 choice for most applicants and highly ATS-friendly.

🎯 Functional

Best for: career changers, gaps in employment

Puts skills front and center instead of dates. Use only when your experience doesn't match the target role.

⚡ Combination / Hybrid

Best for: experienced professionals

Opens with a skills summary, then backs it up with chronological work history. The best of both worlds.

2

Craft a Strong Contact Header

The header is the first thing recruiters see. Make it count with these essentials:

  • Full name — prominently displayed; use a slightly larger font
  • Phone number — one reliable number with a professional voicemail
  • Professional email — firstname.lastname@email.com beats xXcoolname99Xx@email.com
  • LinkedIn URL — customized URL (linkedin.com/in/yourname)
  • Location — city & state/province is enough; no full address needed
  • Portfolio / GitHub — if relevant to your field
💡 Pro tip: Skip photos unless they're standard in your country (e.g., Germany). Photos can confuse ATS systems and introduce unconscious bias.
3

Write a Killer Professional Summary

Replace the outdated "Objective" with a 2–3 sentence Professional Summary that answers: "Why should we keep reading?"

✅ Strong summary

"Results-driven marketing analyst with 4+ years of experience in data-driven campaign optimization. Increased client conversion rates by an average of 35% across 12 accounts through A/B testing and audience segmentation. Seeking to leverage analytics expertise in a senior role at a fast-growing SaaS company."

❌ Weak objective

"Looking for a challenging position where I can grow my skills and contribute to an organization."

🎓 For students / fresh grads: Focus on your degree, relevant coursework, projects, and any internship or volunteer experience. Example: "Computer science student graduating May 2026 with hands-on experience building full-stack web applications and data visualization tools."
4

Describe Experience with Impact (STAR / XYZ Method)

Don't just list duties — show results. Use the XYZ formula:

Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]

✅ Impact-driven bullet

"Reduced API response time by 42% (from 1.2s to 0.7s) by implementing query caching and database indexing, improving user satisfaction scores by 15 points."

❌ Duty-listing bullet

"Responsible for managing the database and fixing bugs."

Power verbs to use:

Architected Automated Delivered Engineered Generated Implemented Increased Launched Led Optimized Reduced Scaled Spearheaded Streamlined
5

Education & Skills Sections

Education: List degrees in reverse chronological order. Include relevant coursework, GPA (if 3.5+), and academic honours.

Skills section — divide into categories:

🔧 Hard Skills

Programming languages, tools, frameworks, certifications. Be specific: say "Python (pandas, Flask)" not just "Python".

🤝 Soft Skills

Leadership, communication, problem-solving. Only include ones you can back up with experience bullets.

🔑 ATS keyword tip: Mirror the exact keywords in the job posting. If they say "React.js", don't just write "React". ATS systems match exact strings.
6

Formatting, ATS & Final Polish

  • Length: 1 page for <10 years experience; 2 pages for 10+ years
  • Font: Use clean fonts — Calibri, Arial, Helvetica, Georgia (10–12pt body, 14–16pt name)
  • Margins: 0.5"–1" on all sides; never below 0.5"
  • File format: Save as PDF unless the posting requests .docx
  • File name: FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf
  • No graphics / tables / columns — many ATS can't parse them
  • Proofread 3 times, then have someone else read it
  • Tailor every resume — generic resumes get generic rejections

Resume Do's & Don'ts

✅ Do

  • Quantify achievements with numbers, %, and $
  • Tailor keywords for each job application
  • Use strong action verbs at the start of every bullet
  • Include a LinkedIn profile link
  • Use consistent tense: past for former roles, present for current
  • Save as PDF with a professional file name

❌ Don't

  • Use "References available upon request" — it's assumed
  • Include personal info like age, marital status, SSN
  • Use fancy fonts, colors, or graphics (unless creative field)
  • Copy-paste job descriptions as your bullet points
  • Lie or exaggerate — background checks are real
  • Use pronouns ("I managed…") — start directly with the verb

Part 2

How to Write a Compelling Cover Letter

A great cover letter is your chance to tell the story behind the resume — why you care about this role, why you're the right fit, and what you'll bring on day one.

1

Research the Company First

Before writing a single word, spend 15–20 minutes researching:

  • The company's mission, values, and recent news
  • The job description — highlight keywords and required skills
  • Who the hiring manager is (check LinkedIn) so you can address them by name
  • The company's tone — is it formal corporate or casual startup?
💡 Pro tip: Reference something specific about the company in your opening. Generic letters that could be sent to any company get ignored.
2

Structure Your Cover Letter

A winning cover letter follows a clear 4-paragraph structure:

📬 Header

Your contact info, date, and the hiring manager's contact info. Match the format of your resume for consistency.

🎣 Opening Paragraph — The Hook

State the exact position, how you found it, and one compelling reason you're excited. Lead with your strongest credential.

💪 Body Paragraphs — The Evidence (1–2 paragraphs)

Pick 2–3 achievements from your resume that directly match the job requirements. Use specific numbers and the STAR method. Explain why these experiences make you perfect for this role.

🏁 Closing Paragraph — The Ask

Reiterate your enthusiasm, briefly summarize your value, and include a clear call-to-action: "I'd love to discuss how my experience can contribute to your team. I'm available for a call at your convenience."

3

Write an Opening That Grabs Attention

✅ Strong opening

"When I saw your posting for a Data Engineer at Spotify, I knew I had to apply — I've spent the past two years building the exact type of real-time data pipelines your team describes, processing 50M+ events daily at my current role."

❌ Weak opening

"I am writing to express my interest in the open position at your company. I believe I would be a good fit."

4

Show, Don't Tell — Use Specific Examples

Instead of saying "I'm a great team player", demonstrate it:

✅ Specific evidence

"At my internship with Acme Corp, I collaborated with a cross-functional team of 8 engineers and 3 designers to ship a customer-facing dashboard 2 weeks ahead of schedule, resulting in a 28% increase in user engagement."

🧮 The magic formula: For each achievement, include what you did, how you did it, and what the measurable result was. Numbers build credibility.
5

Formatting & Final Checklist

  • Length: One page maximum — 3 to 4 paragraphs, 250–400 words
  • Font & style: Match your resume fonts for a cohesive application package
  • Tone: Professional but personable; match the company culture
  • Address by name: "Dear Ms. Johnson" beats "To Whom It May Concern"
  • Sign-off: "Sincerely" or "Best regards" — keep it simple
  • Proofread: Read it aloud, then have someone else review it
  • File format: PDF, named FirstName-LastName-CoverLetter.pdf
  • Customize for every application — yes, every single one

Cover Letter Do's & Don'ts

✅ Do

  • Research the company and reference specifics
  • Address the hiring manager by name when possible
  • Connect your achievements to the job requirements
  • Show genuine enthusiasm for the role and company
  • Keep it to one page — respect the reader's time
  • End with a clear call-to-action

❌ Don't

  • Repeat your resume word-for-word — expand on highlights
  • Start every sentence with "I"
  • Use the same letter for every application
  • Apologize for what you lack ("Although I don't have…")
  • Include salary expectations unless specifically asked
  • Let AI write the whole thing — hiring managers can tell
